ShadowContext is an independent security publication for analysts, engineers, security leaders, and curious builders. We cover the forces changing modern defense: adversary operations, identity abuse, cloud exposure, autonomous systems, and the collision between AI and cybersecurity.

## Our editorial standard

We start with primary sources—security advisories, incident research, technical reports, and disclosures from the teams closest to the event. Then we add the part a feed cannot: context.

Every briefing aims to answer three questions:

1. **What actually changed?** We distinguish observations from forecasts and marketing claims.
2. **Why does it matter?** We connect technical findings to architecture, operations, and risk.
3. **What should defenders do next?** Recommendations are prioritized, bounded, and practical.

## Responsible by design

Our reporting is safe for work and defense-oriented. We avoid sensationalism, graphic material, leaked personal data, and operational detail that would turn analysis into a misuse guide. When facts are uncertain or evolving, we say so.

## Independence and corrections

Vendor research is valuable, but every vendor has a point of view. We link to the original material so readers can inspect the evidence and judge conclusions for themselves. If we get something wrong, send a concise correction to [shadowcontext@gmail.com](mailto:shadowcontext@gmail.com).

> Signal over noise. Context over panic.
