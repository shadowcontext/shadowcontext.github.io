## IOC and threat-feed risks

IOCs and threat feeds are contextual signals, not automatic proof of malicious activity. They can be incomplete, stale, duplicated, mistyped, reassigned, shared by legitimate and malicious infrastructure, or valid only during a limited time window. IP addresses and domains can change ownership or purpose. URLs can become inactive or redirect elsewhere. File hashes identify exact files but do not determine how or why a file exists in a particular environment.

False positives can interrupt legitimate communications, disable services, block customers or suppliers, delete benign files, trigger unnecessary investigations, or otherwise disrupt operations. False negatives can leave malicious activity undetected. Append-only feeds may intentionally retain historical indicators that no longer represent active infrastructure.

You must not treat any ShadowContext IOC as a standalone blocking instruction. Before importing data into a firewall, endpoint product, SIEM, SOAR platform, DNS control, email gateway, detection rule, automated response workflow, or other production system, you are responsible for:

- verifying the original source and its handling or sharing designation;
- assessing recency, confidence, context, and relevance to your environment;
- enriching and scoring the indicator using appropriate internal and external evidence;
- testing proposed detections or controls in an isolated or monitored setting;
- establishing review, exception, expiration, rollback, and recovery procedures; and
- obtaining any technical, managerial, legal, regulatory, or customer approvals required for your use.
