Dubai Police has renewed its warning about online fraudsters offering jobs and visas in exchange for money. The practical lesson is stronger than “be careful online”: recruitment claims should be treated as unverified until their identifiers resolve through an official UAE service reached independently of the original message.

## What the warning establishes

The National reported that Dubai Police’s anti-fraud centre warned of scammers posing as official bodies or using the names of unlicensed companies. According to the report, the approaches are increasingly delivered through social media and messaging apps, where a polished profile, copied branding and a plausible conversation can make a fabricated process feel legitimate.

This is an individual fraud warning with a concrete security consequence. A false recruiter can combine two powerful forms of pressure: the promise of work and the apparent urgency of a visa process. The requested payment may then be framed as an administrative fee rather than as the destination of the scam.

Dubai Police advised the public to complete visa and other important transactions through official channels. It also identified reporting routes for suspected fraud: the Dubai Police smart app, the eCrime platform and the non-emergency number 901. Those channels matter after suspicion arises, but the most useful control comes earlier, before money or sensitive documents move.

## Verify the record, not the presentation

The Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation says a worker must receive an employment offer before the employment relationship begins. Its worker guidance states that approved offer forms carry serial numbers or barcodes that can be checked through the ministry’s website, smart application or call centre. MOHRE also provides an online Offer Inquiry service for employment offers and contracts.

This creates a clean trust boundary. The message, attachment, recruiter profile and phone number are all claims. The record retrieved from a separately opened official service is evidence. Applicants should type the known government address themselves or use a saved official bookmark, then enter the offer details there. A link or telephone number supplied by the recruiter should not be used to verify that same recruiter.

Visa claims require the same separation. The police warning, as reported by The National, directs applicants to the General Directorate of Residency and Foreigners Affairs for permits issued in Dubai and to the Federal Authority for Identity, Citizenship, Customs and Port Security for permits issued in the other emirates. A screenshot of a permit is not equivalent to a record returned by the responsible authority.

## Add friction before payment

The report also notes that employers are responsible for recruitment expenses under UAE labour rules. That makes a request for an applicant to pay recruitment or visa charges a strong stop signal. It does not need to prove fraud by itself; it should halt the transaction until MOHRE or the relevant immigration authority confirms the process through contact details obtained independently.

Individuals should preserve the message, sender details, payment request and any claimed offer number without forwarding suspicious links. They can then check the offer, verify the permit with the correct issuing authority and report the approach if the records fail to match. Sensitive identity documents should be shared only after the recipient and lawful purpose have been verified.

Employers and recruitment teams can reinforce the same control by telling candidates, at the start of every legitimate process, which official identifier they will receive and where to check it. They should also state clearly that unexpected fee requests or a move to an unapproved messaging account must be escalated through a published company contact.

## Make verification independent

The central defensive lesson is independence. Fraud succeeds when the same conversation supplies the opportunity, the urgency, the payment route and the supposed proof. Breaking that loop is more reliable than judging spelling, design quality or the confidence of the caller.

A safe workflow is deliberately simple: pause, leave the conversation, open the appropriate government service independently, and match the offer or permit record before taking the next step. If the claim cannot survive that out-of-band check, no amount of presentation should restore its credibility.
